The idea

Two sections of the same report should tell the same story.

Under the EU's new sustainability reporting rules (CSRD), about 11,700 companies must publish detailed climate disclosures. Most AI tools just pull numbers out of these reports. This one checks whether the numbers agree with each other.

A simple example

The restaurant menu vs. the grocery bill

A restaurant's menu says it's going fully plant-based by next year. Its grocery receipts show it's still buying only meat. Neither document is false on its own, but together they don't add up. That gap is what this tool looks for.

ESRS E1

The climate section: the menu

What the company says it will do: emissions, targets, net-zero year, and the transition levers it's pulling to get there.

EU Taxonomy

The Taxonomy section: the receipts

Where the money actually goes: the share of revenue, capex and opex that meets the EU's technical definition of "green."

Auditors, activist investors and regulators read reports this way. Sustainability teams usually don't, because cross-reading hundreds of pages by hand takes hours. This pipeline does a first pass in about 90 seconds.

How it works

Four steps, three model calls.

Deliberately simple: no vector database, no RAG, no agent framework. The prompts do the work, and every prompt asks for page citations and tells the model not to guess.

01 · Python

Find the sections

Read the PDF and score every page against keyword lists to locate the climate and Taxonomy sections.

305–676 pages → ~20 relevant
02 · Claude

Extract the climate data

Emissions, targets, transition plan, methodology, plus self-reported data quality flags.

→ e1.json
03 · Claude

Extract the Taxonomy data

Eligible and aligned revenue, capex and opex, activity by activity, plus DNSH and safeguards checks.

→ taxonomy.json
04 · Claude

Cross-check them

Do the levers match aligned activities? Do targets match investment? Is one section more rigorous? Returns a verdict, findings and questions.

→ crosscheck.json

      What the comparison shows
      
    The two companies fail in different ways.
    
      
    That's the useful part. Different kinds of companies need different pre-publication checks, and one pipeline catches both.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Unilever · consumer goods · LOW
    The plan doesn't show up in the spending
    • Transition levers in the climate section map to Taxonomy activities that are 0% aligned
    • Two explanations for zero alignment on the same page pull in different directions
    • 88% of "eligible" capex is buildings it owns, not transition investment
    
      
    Schneider Electric · industrial · MEDIUM
    The numbers don't reconcile across sections
    • Itemized transition capex doesn't match the reported total
    • 14% of revenue is non-aligned over chemical compliance, but the climate plan never mentions it
    • OpEx alignment looks high because OpEx is defined narrowly

    Limits and next steps
    What it doesn't do yet
    
    
    
      
    Brittle section finding
    Keyword scoring works on these two formats. Other report layouts need an LLM or layout-based section classifier.
    
      
    Two disclosures, not twelve
    A full CSRD audit spans all 12 ESRS standards plus the financial statements. Same pipeline shape, more pairings.
    
      
    No benchmark yet
    Findings are strong on two companies. Production use needs a labeled set of disclosures with known issues to measure recall.
    
      
    Model output, not verdicts
    Every finding cites pages so a person can check it, as with the Schneider example above.
